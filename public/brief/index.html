1

One thing we settled for you

Resolved from IRS records, 26 August

There is no 2023 deficit. The number circulating is a withdrawn one.

ProPublica’s website and ProPublica’s own API disagree about your 2023 finances. The website has it right; the API — the machine-readable feed other research tools pull from — still returns your original filing, a $460,313 loss with net assets down 85%. We pulled both filings from the IRS to be certain which was which. You filed 2023 twice: an original in October 2024, then an amended return in February 2025 correcting how contribution revenue and program expenses had been reported.

Charity Navigator has picked up the corrected figures. ProPublica’s API had not, as of 1 September 2026, and ProPublica’s own page warns that amended returns “may not be reflected.” So depending on how someone looks you up, they may be reading a deficit you did not have. That is worth fixing at the source, and worth getting ahead of on your own site — which the draft Evidence page now does.

2

The direction, and where you may disagree

Four choices worth explaining before you look at the pages.

01

We made your numbers smaller

This is the single biggest change, and everything else follows from it. Your site currently leads with 1,200+ youth empowered, 7,200+ families reached, 171,000 lives impacted. None of those is a lie — but they are coalition and movement totals, and they sit on a 501(c)(3) whose last filed revenue was under a million dollars. A program officer notices that gap in about ninety seconds. When they cannot reconcile your headline with your Form 990, they stop trusting the rest of the page, including the parts that are rock solid.

Today

  • 1,200+ youth empowered
  • 7,200+ families reached
  • 171,000 lives impacted
  • $50 million invested
  • 35+ years of experience

Impressive, unverifiable, and not yours alone.

Proposed

  • 178 children tutored, 173 finished
  • 8 partner schools, all named
  • 150 volunteers signed up
  • 24 teenagers on a payroll
  • 501(c)(3) since 2011

Smaller, sourced, and checkable by a stranger.

The big numbers are not deleted. They live in a section called Two different numbers, kept apart, in their own column, clearly labeled as what the coalition you convene delivers. You get credit for the scale without paying the credibility tax for blending it with your own service figures.

02

Why literacy leads — and why that might be wrong

The case for it. It is your own stated flagship: listed first, eighth year running, the only program with a dedicated director. Its numbers are your best ones — 178 enrolled and 173 completed comes off a school enrollment roll, which a stranger can audit. One-to-one early reading is a funding category program officers already understand. And six programs need a spine: "one arc, kindergarten to a first paycheck" only reads as an arc if something anchors one end.

01

It may not be your center of gravity. By your own reporting, Jesus Week is far larger — 512 outreaches, 3,900 participants, 402 congregations. Literacy reaches 178 children. A funder who gives on a literacy pitch and later finds a largely evangelistic organization has the exact trust problem this redesign exists to prevent.

02

SYEP may deserve the lead instead. Twenty-four teenagers, twenty-five paid hours a week, third year running, through a City of New York program. It is secular, it is verifiable without asking you for anything, and it currently appears nowhere on your website.

03

A single focus narrows the funder set. Leading with reading optimizes for education money and quietly deprioritizes violence-prevention money, workforce money and family-services money — all of which you do real work in.

Our view

Keep literacy as the visual anchor, but promote Summer Youth Employment to co-lead — we teach them to read, then we put them to work. It tells the whole kindergarten-to-payroll arc, opens workforce funding, and leans on the one program a funder can verify through the City rather than through you. We would rather hear your view than assume ours.

03

What we left out, and why

Game Changers

Cut entirely

The page claims a 60% decrease in dropout rates and a 70% improvement in student behavior, with no source, baseline, sample or method attached. This is the highest-risk item on the current site: a program officer who cannot source one number starts doubting all of them. We would rather reinstate it with a real evaluation behind it than run it as is.

Christmas / Winter Wonderland

Held back

Genuinely large by reach, and we would like it on the site. We held it because the toy count falls from roughly 25,000 to 7,000 between the 2023 and 2024 reports with no explanation, and an unexplained 72% drop invites the wrong question. Give us one sentence of context and it goes back in.

Care Portal

Demoted

Moved out of the six-program grid to a partner credential, because it is a network you participate in rather than a program you staff. That may undersell it — it is also the clearest link between your work and the wider Safe Harbor collective. Easy to promote back if you want it counted as a program.

04

Why we published your weak spots

This will be the most uncomfortable part of the draft, so here is the reasoning in full. The Evidence page states plainly that you have no independent audit, no adopted records-retention policy, no external evaluation, and that you amended your 2023 return.

None of that is a secret. Charity Navigator already publishes the audit and retention findings, and rates you one star at 50% largely because of them. Any program officer runs that search before the first meeting. The information is going to reach them either way.

The only variable is whether they hear it from you first, next to a dated plan to fix it — or discover it in week three and quietly wonder what else they were not told. The first version reads as an organization with its eyes open. The second reads as a risk. Almost no nonprofit does this. That is exactly why it works.

It is a trade, not a confession

Every gap on the page sits beside a "by end of 2026" column. You are not admitting failure; you are publishing a work plan and inviting someone to fund it.

The ask becomes concrete

"Fund our first external evaluation" is a fundable sentence. "Support our mission" is not.

If you disagree, say so

This is a strategy, not a rule. It is your organization and your reputation, and the page can be softened or removed. We would just want to talk it through first.

3

What we need from you

Every item is either a blank we deliberately left on the draft, a place where two of your own sources disagree, a document the site promises and does not yet have, or a question raised by the new Get Involved page.

A

Blocking — the site should not come out of draft until these land

  1. 1
    Resolved — needs your words

    Your plain-English account of the FY2023 restatement — and the amended 990 as a PDF.

    The amended return is the good version, but your Schedule O says only that revenue and program expenses "were incorrectly reported on original tax return filed," and any program officer who sees an amended 990 will ask what happened. We need one or two sentences we can publish, plus the amended return as a PDF for the funder packet.

  2. 2
    Held back — nowhere a funder will see it

    An explanation of the FY2023 functional-expense split.

    The operative amended return reports program services $331,591 (46.5%), management & general $382,047 (53.5%), and fundraising $0. Management spending exceeds program spending, and most funders screen at 65–75% program. The superseded original had shown 83.8% program — the amendment is what moved it.

    Either the allocation is wrong, or there is a reason for it: a build-out year, shared costs booked centrally. It is nowhere on the draft site, and it is the single most damaging number in your public record. We want your account before deciding how the site handles it. Related: $0 fundraising expense against $794,141 in contributions is itself a flag.

  3. 3

    Aggregator cleanup — a decision.

    ProPublica's web page shows the corrected figures, but its API still serves the withdrawn deficit, and anything built on that API inherits a loss you did not have. Someone should ask ProPublica to refresh, and the amended 990 should be posted on your own site so the correct version is the one that ranks.

  4. 4

    Cost per child, per year — and cost to adopt one school.

    Deliberately left blank. It is the first thing a program officer calculates, and if you don't publish it they will derive it from the wrong two numbers. The single highest-value figure missing today.

  5. 5

    A shared inbox address — hello@ or grants@.

    Every contact route on the current site is one staff member's personal email. The draft shows a placeholder rather than publish a fake one. This now blocks the Get Involved page too: its four forms have nowhere to deliver to until this exists.

  6. 6
    Partly answered by the 990 itself

    Sign-off on the accountability and governance rows.

    Your FY2023 990 states that the board reviews the 990 before filing, that directors complete an annual conflict-of-interest disclosure, and that governing documents are available on request. Charity Navigator's "no records retention policy" and "no independent audit" flags are separate and still stand. Confirm all of it, and give us real dates for the "by end of 2026" column.

  7. 7

    Board and staff rosters — plus consent to publish names.

    The draft deliberately shows the board as a structure — nine directors, two of whom are staff — rather than naming real people on a page you have not approved. The same question applies to a staff page: we would like one, and we are not going to publish anyone’s name or photograph until you tell us who has agreed to it.

  8. 8

    Photo permission.

    The draft reuses four photographs from your current site. Confirm we may use them, and whether any child pictured lacks a media release.

D

The Get Involved page

Added 31 August. The page is built and live on the draft; these three answers decide whether it stays as drafted.

  1. 20
    Both programs exist — corrected 1 September

    The internship has no page, and your STM intake already has a form.

    An earlier draft of this item said we could not find either program. That was wrong — we had searched the raw HTML of a Wix site, which renders its content in the browser. Both are real and both are on your current site.

    Short-term missions has its own page and its own nav item, and its intake already runs through a JotForm ("Short Term Missions Intake Form"). Tell us whether Get Involved should point at that form or replace it — we would rather not run two intakes for one program.

    The internship is the broken one. You promote it on the home page as a 10-month program with several paragraphs behind it, but every "Internship" button on the site links back to /home, and there is no internship page anywhere — we tried five likely URLs and all of them 404. Anyone who reads that copy and wants to apply has nowhere to go. We have drafted a section for it; we need the real terms — dates, hours, housing, stipend, whether it carries credit — to fill it in.

  2. 21

    Where should form submissions go?

    All four forms are deliberately inert — submitting shows a plain notice saying the draft is not connected and nothing was sent. We would rather collect nothing than collect real names and email addresses, possibly including volunteers who will work with children, on a site you have not approved. Give us the destination (see ask 5) and we will wire them up.

  3. 22

    Confirm giving stays where it is.

    You already take donations through Wix, with PayPal and Stripe behind it, and the draft simply links out to that page rather than rebuilding it. That is the right call as long as you stay on Wix. If this redesign ever replaces your current site, giving needs a real migration — a processor, your bank details, and receipting — which is a separate piece of work, not a page.

4

Seven questions, and then we build

These are genuinely open. Your answers change the design, not just the wording.

  1. Where is the gravity of your future programming?

    We led with literacy because that is where your evidence is strongest today — named schools, enrollment counts, a measurable outcome. But that is a read of your past two years, not a statement about your next five. If the center of gravity is moving — toward the summer jobs work, toward Care Portal, toward the Cities of Joy church-planting frame, toward something not on the site at all — tell us now.

    It is the one piece of feedback that would change the structure of the site rather than its wording.

  2. Does literacy lead, or does it share the lead with the summer jobs program?

    SYEP is your most fundable and most independently verifiable work — a City program, third year running — and it appears nowhere on your current site.

  3. Is the split between Urban Hope's own numbers and the coalition's drawn in the right place?

  4. Are you willing to publish the accountability table as drafted — gaps, 2026 plan and all?

  5. Is the faith framing right?

    Present and plainly stated, with an explicit line between school-day tutoring in DOE buildings and the Bible clubs.

  6. Should Game Changers, Christmas and Care Portal come back in — and on what evidence?

  7. Who is the real audience?

    One named foundation you are about to approach, or every funder at once? A named target would sharpen the whole site considerably.
